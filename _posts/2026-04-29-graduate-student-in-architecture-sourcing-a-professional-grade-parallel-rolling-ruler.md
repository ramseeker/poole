## Essential Shopping List

- **Professional Parallel Rolling Ruler (12”-18” minimum)**
- **Replacement/Spare Roller Assemblies (2-3 sets)**
- **Drafting Surface Mat (self-healing, A2 or larger)**
- **T-Square or Drafting Triangle (for orthogonal checks)**
- **Roller Lubricant (silicon-based, non-staining)**
- **Ruler Edge Protector (thin, adhesive tape)**
- **Carrying Case or Sleeve (rigid, padded)**

## Detailed Buying Guide

### **Professional Parallel Rolling Ruler (12”-18” minimum)**
The core tool. For architecture graduate work—studio reviews, pin-ups, technical drawings, and quick iterations—you need a ruler that tracks *parallel* without slipping or drifting. **Look for a model with a clear acrylic or anodized aluminum body** (acrylic is lighter, aluminum is more rigid under pressure). The rolling mechanism should use **sealed ball bearings or precision-engineered nylon rollers**; avoid cheap plastic rollers that will flatten or collect dust. A **12” length** is portable for sketchbooks, but an **18” ruler** handles full-size trace paper rolls and drafting boards. **Double-check that the roller track is recessed**—this prevents ink smears and protects the rollers when you set the ruler down. Brand recommendations: **Alvin, Pickett, or Koh-I-Noor** are studio-tested standards.

### **Replacement/Spare Roller Assemblies (2-3 sets)**
Rollers are the first component to fail. Grad students roll rulers across heavy trace, vellum, and foam core—shifting graphite and eraser dust grinds down the bearing surfaces. **Buy genuine OEM replacements** for your specific ruler model; generic “universal” rollers often have incompatible diameters or axle lengths. **Why spare sets?** One set can be on the ruler, one in your bag, and one stored for mid-semester panic when a roller seizes during a final crit. **Inspect the roller material**: brass or stainless steel rolling pins last longer than plastic, but some premium rulers use **ceramic-coated bearings** for near-frictionless glide.

### **Drafting Surface Mat (self-healing, A2 or larger)**
Even if your studio has communal tables, a personal mat is non-negotiable. **A self-healing mat** (e.g., **Olfa, Dahle, or Alvin**) protects your rolling ruler’s rollers from table grit and prevents the ruler from scratching/damaging your workspace. **Why A2 or larger?** Architecture drawings often exceed letter size; a mat large enough to layer trace paper over technical drawings keeps your ruler from hanging off the edge, which causes uneven rolling and false parallel lines. **Thickness matters**: a 3mm mat is adequate, but 5-6mm mats provide better stability for heavy rulers and repeated rolling.

### **T-Square or Drafting Triangle (for orthogonal checks)**
A parallel rolling ruler is not a replacement for a T-square—it’s a *complement*. **Reason**: No matter how well-calibrated, your rolling ruler’s parallel accuracy decreases over time due to roller wear, warped edges, or adhesive residue on the track. **Use a draft triangle (45/90 or 30/60/90) or a sturdy T-square to periodically verify that your ruler’s edge is perfectly perpendicular to the grid direction on your mat.** This is critical during schematic design phases when a 0.5° drift becomes a 1/8” error over a 3-foot line. **Pro tip**: choose a **clear acrylic triangle** (e.g., **Staedtler Mars**) so you can see reference lines underneath.

### **Roller Lubricant (silicon-based, non-staining)**
Dry rollers = jerky movement and skipped lines. **Never use WD-40 or oil-based lubricants**—they can cloud acrylic, discolor paper, and attract dust. **A silicon-based spray or precision oil (e.g., **Super Lube 51010** or **Dritz Sewing Machine Oil**) applied sparingly to the roller axles (not the rolling surface) keeps the mechanism smooth. **Application method**: one tiny drop per axle, then roll the ruler 10-15 times on scrap paper to distribute. Over-lubrication leaves residue on drawings; under-lubrication causes noise and wear.

### **Ruler Edge Protector (thin, adhesive tape)**
Parallel rolling rulers have exposed edges that chip, nick, or become uneven when dropped on concrete studio floors. **A 1/4” wide, crystal-clear polypropylene tape (like **3M Scotch 845** or **book repair tape**) applied to the *non-rolling edges* of the ruler** prevents damage without adding bulk. **Critical for grad school**: damaged edges cause inconsistent line weights and inaccurate parallel travel. Replace the tape every 2-3 months or immediately after a hard drop.

### **Carrying Case or Sleeve (rigid, padded)**
You will transport this ruler daily—to studio, to the print shop, to pin-up reviews. **A soft sleeve is insufficient**; parallel rulers are brittle under pressure (especially acrylic models) and the rollers can be jarred out of alignment. **A rigid case with interior foam cutouts (e.g., **Pelican 1050** or an inexpensive **Flambeau UniBox**) protects the ruler from compression, drops, and grit. **If budget-strapped**, use a **heavy-duty manila envelope padded with foam sheets**—but test for rigidity; the ruler should not flex under the weight of your backpack.
