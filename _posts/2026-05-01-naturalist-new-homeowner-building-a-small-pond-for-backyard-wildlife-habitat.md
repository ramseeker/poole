## Essential Shopping List

- **Pond liner** (EPDM rubber or PVC, 45-mil thickness minimum; size calculated as: length + (2x depth) + 1 ft overlap per side)
- **Underlayment fabric** (non-woven geotextile, at least 6 oz/sq yd)
- **Submersible pump** (rated for pond volume: pump flow = pond volume ÷ 2, per hour)
- **Mechanical filter** (foam block or filter mat, sized to pump output)
- **Biological filter media** (lava rock, ceramic rings, or Bio-Balls)
- **Aeration stone or air pump** (for deep ponds over 18 inches)
- **Edging stones** (flat, irregular flagstone or river rock, 4-8 inch diameter)
- **Aquatic plants:** (1) submerged oxygenators (e.g., *Anacharis*, *Hornwort*), (2) marginals (e.g., *Pickerelweed*, *Iris versicolor*), (3) floating (e.g., *Frogbit*, *Duckweed*)
- **Hardy water lily** or *Lotus* tuber (for surface cover + shade)
- **Beneficial bacteria starter** (liquid or dry, *Nitrosomonas*/*Nitrobacter* blend)
- **Mosquito dunks** (Bti-based, *Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis*)
- **pH/Alkalinity test kit** (liquid drop style, not strips)
- **Dechlorinator** (sodium thiosulfate-based, for tap water fill)
- **Small pond net** (fine mesh, 18-inch handle)
- **Substrate** (pea gravel or washed construction sand, 1-2 inch layer, no limestone)
- **Optional: Solar-powered fountain pump** (for shallow wildlife water bird bath area)

## Buying Guide

### Pond Liner & Underlayment
**Logic:** Naturalist ponds require a flexible, durable barrier that conforms to irregular shapes without puncturing. **EPDM (ethylene propylene diene monomer)** rubber is the gold standard—it’s UV-resistant, flexible in cold winters, and safe for aquatic life. Avoid pre-formed rigid liners; they limit wildlife access and shallow shelves. **45-mil thickness** prevents roots from sedges or willows from piercing. **Underlayment** is non-negotiable: it protects the liner from sharp rocks and burrowing crayfish. Choose **non-woven geotextile** (not woven) because it resists tearing and allows gas exchange under the liner.

### Pump & Filtration
**Logic:** Stagnant water breeds mosquitoes and anaerobic bacteria. A **submersible pump** with a flow rate that turns over the pond volume every 2 hours (e.g., 200-gallon pond = 100 gph pump) prevents stagnation without creating strong currents that discourage dragonfly larvae or amphibians. **Mechanical filter** (foam block) removes leaf litter and sediment before it decays. **Biological filter media** provides surface area for nitrogen-cycle bacteria that convert fish waste (if added) and decaying plant matter into less toxic nitrates. For a wildlife pond under 100 gallons, a simple sponge filter over the pump intake suffices; skip expensive canister filters.

### Air Pump & Aeration Stone
**Logic:** Deep ponds (over 18 inches) can stratify and lose oxygen in summer heat, causing fish kills. An **air stone** connected to a **low-wattage air pump** (0.5–1.5 watts) adds dissolved oxygen without disturbing the shallow littoral zone where tadpoles and insects hide. Place the stone in the deepest spot. For tiny ponds (under 50 gallons), aeration is optional if you have lots of submerged plants.

### Edging Stones
**Logic:** Wildlife needs gentle, sloped access in and out of the water—especially frogs, salamanders, and beetles. **Flat flagstone** or **river rock** placed unevenly around the liner’s edge creates micro-terraces. Use stones that weigh at least 5 pounds each to prevent raccoons from dislodging them. Leave 1-inch gaps between stones for soil and moss; this creates crevices for isopods and insects.

### Aquatic Plants (The Living Filter)
**Logic:** Plants are the pond’s true filtration system. **Submerged oxygenators** (e.g., *Hornwort*, *Elodea*) outcompete algae for nutrients and produce oxygen during daylight. **Marginal plants** (e.g., *Blue Flag Iris*, *Pickerelweed*) soak up excess nutrients from runoff and provide egg-laying sites for damselflies. **Floating plants** (*Frogbit*, *Duckweed*) shade the water surface, reducing evaporation and inhibiting green water algae. **Water lilies** are critical: their broad leaves offer tadpoles shade from herons and dragonfly perches. *Note: Avoid invasive species like *Purple Loosestrife* or *Water Hyacinth*—they require native equivalents for true naturalism.*

### Beneficial Bacteria & Mosquito Control
**Logic:** New ponds lack the natural bacterial colonies that break down fish waste and fallen leaves. A **starter dose** of nitrifying bacteria (look for *Nitrosomonas* and *Nitrobacter*) speeds establishment by 4–6 weeks. **Mosquito dunks** (Bti) are a non-toxic biological control that kills mosquito larvae but spares dragonfly nymphs and tadpoles. Use one quarter of a dunk per 100 gallons monthly; re-dunk after heavy rain.

### Water Test Kit & Dechlorinator
**Logic:** Tap water often has chlorine, chloramines, and high pH (8.0+), which can kill sensitive frog embryos and aquatic insects. A **liquid drop test kit** (API Pond Master Kit) measures pH, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate—critical for tracking the pond’s maturation. **Dechlorinator** neutralizes chlorine instantly; dose before filling or add per instructions if topping off. For a wildlife pond, target pH 6.5–7.5 and 0 ppm ammonia.

### Pond Net
**Logic:** A **fine-mesh net** (1/8-inch mesh) is essential for removing invasive algae like *blanketweed* (filamentous algae) without scooping up tadpoles. Also use it to fish out fallen leaves in autumn before they decompose and release tannins. Avoid nylon nets that fray; choose polyester or aluminum-handled styles.

### Substrate (Pea Gravel or Sand)
**Logic:** Bare pond liner looks unnatural and provides no grip for benthic organisms (caddisflies, snails). A **1–2 inch layer** of pea gravel or washed sand anchors plant roots, traps organic debris for decomposition, and gives microscopic life a surface for biofilm growth. **Never** use limestone or crushed coral—they raise pH to lethal levels for amphibians.

### Solar Fountain (Optional)
**Logic:** A small **solar-powered fountain** placed in a shallow (2–3 inch) separate basin attracts birds and bees for drinking and bathing without mixing with the main pond that could trap fledglings. Choose a model with a pre-filter sponge to keep debris out. Position it in full sun, 10 feet from the main pond to avoid muddying the liner.
