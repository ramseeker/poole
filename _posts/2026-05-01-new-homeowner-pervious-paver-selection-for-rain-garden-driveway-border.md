## Essential Shopping List for a Rain Garden Driveway Border with Previous Pavers

- **Pervious Pavers (concrete or clay)**
- **Gravel Base (3/4-inch washed angular stone, #57 or #8)**
- **Gravel choker layer (1/2-inch washed stone)**
- **Edging restraints (flexible steel or rigid plastic)**
- **Landscape fabric (nonwoven geotextile)**
- **Stone for joints (1/4-inch washed pea gravel or crushed granite)**
- **Rain garden plants (native, deep-rooted perennials and grasses)**
- **Mulch (shredded hardwood or arborist chips)**
- **Soil amendment (compost or organic planting mix)**
- **Compactor (hand tamper or plate compactor)**
- **String line, stakes, and level**
- **Shovel, rake, and rubber mallet**

## Buying Guide: The Logic Behind Each Choice

### **Pervious Pavers: The Structural Top Layer**
Pervious pavers are the visible, traffic-bearing surface. They’re designed with gaps (typically 10–20% of the surface area) or are themselves porous to allow water to pass through into the base below.

- **Why it matters:** Standard concrete or asphalt is impervious, forcing rainwater to run off your driveway into streets and storm drains. Pervious pavers capture this water, letting it slowly infiltrate into the ground, recharging groundwater and filtering pollutants.
- **Material choice:**
  - **Concrete pavers:** Durable, affordable, and available in many shapes (e.g., rectangular, interlocking). Look for units with ASTM C936 certification for freeze-thaw resistance.
  - **Clay pavers:** More expensive but offer a richer color and higher strength. They resist staining and are excellent for high-traffic borders.
- **Sizing:** For a driveway border, 60mm–80mm thickness is ideal. Thicker pavers (80mm) can handle occasional vehicle weight, while 60mm works for pedestrian-only borders.

### **Gravel Base and Choker Layer: The Infiltration Engine**
- **3/4-inch washed angular stone (e.g., #57 or #8):** This is the storage reservoir beneath the pavers. Water that passes through the surface is temporarily held in the voids of this crushed stone before slowly seeping into the soil below. **Angular stone** locks together, providing structural stability under traffic, while the voids (about 40% of volume) store water. Do **not** use rounded river rock—it shifts under load and compacts unevenly.
- **Choker layer (1/2-inch washed stone):** Placed directly on top of the base, this 1–2 inch layer fills surface irregularities, creates a smooth bedding for the pavers, and prevents the joint material from falling into the base voids. It also improves lateral drainage.

### **Edging Restraints: Preventing Pavers from Spreading**
A pervious paver border, especially along a driveway, needs rigid containment to keep pavers from shifting apart under vehicle weight or frost heave.

- **Flexible steel edging:** Best for straight runs or gentle curves. It’s durable, invisible once installed, and won’t rot or bend out of shape. Look for 1/8-inch thick, galvanized steel with pre-drilled holes for stakes.
- **Rigid plastic edging:** Cheaper but less forgiving. Suitable for light-duty borders (pedestrian-only). Avoid low-cost “lawn edging” that can bow under vehicle weight.

### **Landscape Fabric: The Separation Layer**
Place **nonwoven geotextile fabric** between the gravel base and the native subsoil. This crucial layer stops soil particles from migrating upward into the stone voids over time, preventing clogging and preserving infiltration rate.

- **Why not woven fabric?** Woven fabrics are for erosion control, not separation. Nonwoven fabrics (typically 4–6 oz/sq yd) allow water to pass while blocking silt and clay.
- **Application:** Cut it to span the entire width of the paver border + 6 inches on each side. Overlap seams by 12 inches.

### **Stone for Joints: The Functional Filler**
The gaps between pervious pavers need a material that stays permeable but prevents the pavers from shifting.

- **1/4-inch washed pea gravel or crushed granite:** These have enough interlock to hold pavers in place yet remain open enough for rapid water drainage. **Avoid polymeric sand** (used for standard patio pavers)—it contains glue that hardens and seals the joints, ruining the pervious function.

### **Rain Garden Plants and Mulch: The Biological Cleaners**
The rain garden border isn’t just decorative; it’s a living filtration system.

- **Native deep-rooted perennials:** Roots create channels in the soil for water infiltration. Good choices for sunny areas: **Black-eyed Susan, Switchgrass, Joe-Pye Weed, or Blue Flag Iris**. For shade: **Fern species, Cardinal Flower, or Turtlehead**.
- **Avoid invasive species** (e.g., purple loosestrife, Japanese knotweed) that can clog the system or escape into the landscape.
- **Mulch:** Use **shredded hardwood** or **arborist chips** (not bark nuggets, which float away). Mulch protects soil, suppresses weeds, and slowly decomposes, adding organic matter. Apply 2–3 inches thick—no thicker, or it can create a hydrophobic layer.

### **Soil Amendment: Improving Native Dirt**
Most suburban soils are compacted from construction. For the rain garden to work, the soil must drain.

- **Compost or organic planting mix:** Mix 20–30% into the native soil in the planting areas. This boosts microbial activity, improves drainage in clay, and helps retain moisture in sand.
- **Do not** change the soil profile drastically—the rain garden relies on the existing soil’s infiltration rate (test it: fill a hole with water; if it drains less than 1/2 inch per hour, consider altering plant selection or increasing the gravel base depth).

### **Compactor: The Key to Longevity**
Uncompacted gravel settles over time, creating dips, ponding, and shifting pavers.

- **Hand tamper:** For areas under 100 sq ft, a 25-lb hand tamper is sufficient. Tamp the base in 4–6-inch lifts.
- **Plate compactor:** For larger borders or driveways, rent a plate compactor (3000–5000 lbs of force). **Crucial:** run the compactor wet (spray water on the stone) to achieve maximum density without crushing the pavers. Never compact the top paver surface dry—this can crack them.

### **Installation Tools: Precision Matters**
- **String line and stakes:** Ensure the border is straight and at the correct slope (1–2% away from the house, toward the rain garden or downspout).
- **Level and rubber mallet:** Level each paver individually. A 4-ft level ensures proper drainage slope.
- **Shovel and rake:** For excavating the base trench (depth = paver thickness + choker + base + fabric + 2–4 inches for soil compaction). A wide bow rake is best for spreading gravel.
