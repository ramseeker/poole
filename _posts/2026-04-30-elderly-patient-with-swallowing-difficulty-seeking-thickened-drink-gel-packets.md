## Essential Shopping List for Thickened Drink Gel Packets

- **Pre-thickened, single-serve gel packets** (e.g., SimplyThick, Thick-It Clear Advantage, or Resource ThickenUp Clear)
- **Instant powdered thickener** (reserve for backup or travel)
- **Measuring spoon set** (1/4 tsp, 1/2 tsp, 1 tsp) – only if using powder
- **Clear, microwave-safe cup** (marked with fluid ounces)
- **Spill-proof, wide-brimmed cup or sippy cup** (designed for thickened liquids)
- **Reusable silicone straw** (cut to half length for easier suction)
- **Disposable, pre-measured mixing containers** (optional for caregivers)
- **Portable cooler bag & ice packs** (for storing opened packets)

## ## Buying Guide: Logic Behind Each Item

### **1. Pre-thickened Single-Serve Gel Packets**

**Why this is the foundation:** For an elderly patient with dysphagia, consistency and safety are non-negotiable. Pre-thickened packets eliminate the risk of human error in mixing, which can lead to either too-thin (choking hazard) or too-thick (increased risk of dehydration) liquids. Choose **clear gels** (e.g., Thick-It Clear Advantage) if the patient also takes oral medications, as they are starch-based and less likely to interfere with drug absorption. **Fiber-enriched options** (like SimplyThick) are ideal for patients with constipation, a common comorbidity.

- **Key considerations:** Look for packets that specify “nectar-thick” (most common for moderate difficulty) or “honey-thick” (for severe difficulty). Avoid brands with carrageenan or excessive artificial sweeteners if the patient has GI sensitivities.
- **Storage:** Unopened packets last up to 24 months at room temperature. Opened packets must be refrigerated and used within 24 hours.

### **2. Instant Powdered Thickener (Backup)**

**Why you need a backup:** Gel packets are perishable once opened, and the patient may need a quick thickener for beverages outside the home (e.g., at appointments). Choose a **starch-based powder** (like Thick-It Original) for clear liquids, or a **xanthan gum-based powder** (like SimplyThick) for dairy or acidic drinks (juice, coffee). Avoid powders with the prefix “pre-thickened” – they are not the same as packets.

**Logic:** Powder allows customization of thickness per drink, but requires a caregiver to measure precisely. A digital kitchen scale (1-gram increments) is far more accurate than spoons for this.

### **3. Measuring Spoon Set (1/4 tsp, 1/2 tsp, 1 tsp)**

**Why these spoons matter:** If using powder, even a 1/8 tsp error can shift a thin liquid to a paste-like consistency, making it dangerous. Stainless steel spoons (not plastic) are essential because static attracts powder, causing dosing inaccuracy. Use only **level** scoops (sweep with a knife) – never “heaping.”

- **Pro tip:** Pre-measure powder into small, labeled containers (e.g., pill organizers) for a single shift to reduce caregiver burden.

### **4. Clear, Microwave-Safe Cup (Marked with Fluid Ounces)**

**Why this cup is critical:** Gel packets require mixing with water or another liquid at specific ratios (e.g., 1 packet per 4 oz). A cup with visible markings (etched, not printed) ensures the correct volume. A **microwave-safe** cup allows for warming (if the patient prefers room-temperature drinks), but never microwave the packet itself. The cup should be **wide-bottomed** to prevent tipping; elderly patients with tremors often knock over narrow cups.

### **5. Spill-Proof, Wide-Brimmed Cup or Sippy Cup**

**Why not a regular cup?** Dysphagia often coexists with motor weakness or dementia. A regular cup requires tilting the head back, which opens the airway and increases aspiration risk. Use a **no-spill cup with a valve** (like a medical-grade sippy cup) or a **wide-brimmed cup** (e.g., an insulated mug) with a **two-handle design**. The brim should be at least 3 inches wide to allow the patient to sip without tilting.

- **Material:** Avoid glass. Use BPA-free Tritan or medical-grade silicone.

### **6. Reusable Silicone Straw (Cut to Half Length)**

**Why cut the straw?** Full-length straws force the patient to tilt their head back – the exact motion that promotes aspiration. A straw cut to 4–6 inches encourages an upright, forward-head posture. **Silicone** is soft and won’t damage dentures or sensitive gums. Pair with a **one-way valve** (like a dysphagia straw adapter) to prevent liquid from falling back into the cup.

### **7. Disposable Pre-Measured Mixing Containers (Optional)**

**Why for caregivers?** If you are a caregiver who prepares multiple drinks at once, these containers (empty, single-dose packets with printed volume lines) reduce chaos. Fill with water, add the gel packet, shake, and refrigerate. This prevents the mental load of measuring each time.

### **8. Portable Cooler Bag & Ice Packs**

**Why this is not optional:** Once a gel packet is opened, it must be consumed within 2 hours at room temperature or 24 hours refrigerated. Bacteria multiply rapidly in thickeners. For appointments, grocery trips, or outings, a small insulated bag (not a lunchbox – too bulky for a senior) with two slim ice packs keeps packets safe. Use **reusable ice packs** that conform to the shape of the bag (not hard cubes).

## Crucial Safety Reminders (Not a List Item, But Critical Logic)

- **Never mix gel packets with hot liquids** (above 140°F). Heat deactivates the gelling agent, causing the drink to thin dangerously after cooling.
- **Test the thickness** before serving: Use the “cup tilt test” – if the liquid slides out immediately, it’s too thin; if it drops as a slow wave, it’s nectar-thick; if it clings to the cup before moving, it’s honey-thick.
- **Consult a speech-language pathologist (SLP)** before purchasing any product. The patient’s swallow study results dictate whether nectar-thick (1–1.5 psi) or honey-thick (2–3 psi) is required. A generic packet may be too thick or too thin for their specific diagnosis.
