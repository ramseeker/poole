## Shopping List: Essential Items for Migraine-Sensitive Teens

- **Blue-light-blocking glasses** (amber or orange-tinted lenses with 90%+ blue-light blockage)
- **Tinted notebook** (soft yellow, pink, or sepia pages; non-glare finish)
- **Migraine-safe LED desk lamp** (dimmable, 2700K–3000K warm tone)
- **Gel pen set** (black ink, fine tip, low-friction flow)
- **Screen filter** (for laptop/tablet: matte finish, blue-light reducing)
- **Aluminum water bottle** (insulated, to keep ice-cold water on hand)

## ## Buying Guide: Why Each Item Matters

### ### Blue-Light-Blocking Glasses

**Logic:** Migraine sensitivity often triggers from blue-spectrum light (450–495 nm), which is emitted heavily by screens, fluorescent lights, and even natural daylight. Blue light stimulates the trigeminal nerve and can worsen photophobia. Amber or orange-tinted lenses **filter out 90–99% of blue light** while allowing enough visible light to function. Choose **low-color-distortion lenses** (e.g., FL-41 tint) for indoor use; avoid dark sunglasses (they dim vision, increasing eye strain when reading). **Frame fit** is crucial for teens: look for lightweight, adjustable temples, and a wraparound style to block peripheral light.

**Key features:**  
- Lens tint: amber (daytime) or orange/red (for severe attacks)  
- Lens material: polycarbonate (impact-resistant, lightweight)  
- UVA/UVB protection (bonus for outdoor use)  
- Anti-reflective coating (reduces glare from screens)

**Price range:** $20–$80 (avoid ultra-cheap $5 “blue blockers” that block only 10–20% of blue light—they offer minimal relief)

### ### Tinted Notebook

**Logic:** White paper reflects high-intensity light and can cause a “flicker” effect under artificial lighting, triggering migraines. A **tinted notebook** (soft yellow, beige, or pink) diffuses glare and reduces contrast between ink and page. This lowers visual stress and fatigue during note-taking. Prefer **non-bleeding, matte pages** to avoid ink reflection. Why pink? Studies show pink tint can calm the autonomic nervous system for some migraineurs. Yellow/amber mimics the effect of blue-blocking glasses on paper.

**Key features:**  
- Page tint: pastel yellow (#FFFACD), sepia, or soft pink (avoid bright neon)  
- Paper weight: 120 gsm or higher (prevents ghosting with gel pens)  
- Binding: lay-flat spiral or disc-bound (reduces wrist strain and light gaps)  
- Grid/dotted layout: helps with visual alignment without harsh lines

**Price range:** $10–$30 (spiral-bound from brands like *Rhodia* or *Leuchtturm1917* offer tinted notepads)

### ### Migraine-Safe LED Desk Lamp

**Logic:** Standard overhead lights and cool-white LEDs emit flickering blue light that can instantly trigger a migraine. A **dimmable, warm-temperature lamp** (2700–3000K) provides soft, amber-like illumination. The ability to reduce brightness **eliminates the flashy strobe effect** of flickering LEDs. Look for **flicker-free** certification (e.g., “flicker-free LED” or “PWM-free”). A gooseneck design allows you to aim light away from your eyes toward the notebook page only.

**Key features:**  
- Color temperature: 2700K (warm) for reading; 3000K for task lighting  
- Dimming: continuous (not stepped) to fine-tune brightness  
- Glare shield: frosted diffuser covers the LED chip  
- USB-C rechargeable (avoids wall-wart cord clutter)

**Price range:** $25–$60 (e.g., BenQ ScreenBar or Verilux HappyLight)

### ### Gel Pen Set

**Logic:** Cheap ballpoint pens require heavy pressure, causing hand fatigue and tension that can trigger migraine attacks. **Gel pens** have fluid ink that glides with minimal effort, reducing grip-induced headache. Use **black ink on tinted paper**—black provides high contrast without the harsh blue-white reflection of blue ink. Fine tips (0.5 mm or 0.7 mm) produce clean lines that don’t blur into the tinted paper.

**Key features:**  
- Ink type: gel (low-pressure flow)  
- Tip size: 0.5–0.7 mm (avoid 1.0 mm for small tinted grid paper)  
- Grip: rubberized or ergonomic (reduces pinch stress)  
- Quick-drying (prevents smudging on tinted paper)

**Price range:** $8–$20 for a 5-pack (e.g., Pentel Energel or Pilot G2)

### ### Screen Filter (Laptop/Tablet)

**Logic:** Even with blue-blocking glasses, direct glare from a glossy screen can cause eyestrain and migraine onset. A **matte screen filter** reduces surface reflection by 80–90% and adds an extra layer of blue-light filtering. This is especially useful for school computers where you can’t adjust native color settings. **Size matters**: cut or buy the exact model for your device (or use a universal sheet with adhesive strips). Avoid privacy filters (they alter brightness distribution).

**Key features:**  
- Finish: matte (frosted) not glossy  
- Blue-light filtration: 25–40% (complement, not replace, glasses)  
- Anti-glare: optical coating that diffuses overhead lights  
- Removable/reusable: static cling (no sticky residue)

**Price range:** $15–$40 (e.g., Tech Armor matte screen protectors)

### ### Insulated Water Bottle

**Logic:** Dehydration is a major migraine trigger, and cold drinks can soothe vestibular symptoms. An **insulated bottle** keeps water ice-cold for 12+ hours, even in a backpack under a classroom desk. The metallic or matte finish reduces light reflection (unlike clear plastic bottles that glare). Choose **wide-mouth** for easy ice cube insertion and cleaning.

**Key features:**  
- Capacity: 24–32 oz (school-day size)  
- Material: double-wall stainless steel (no condensation)  
- Sleeve: silicone or neoprene (sound-dampening, if dropped)  
- Lid: twist-top with leakproof seal (avoid straw lids—can trigger cold sensitivity in teeth/jaw)

**Price range:** $15–$35 (e.g., Hydro Flask or Takeya)
