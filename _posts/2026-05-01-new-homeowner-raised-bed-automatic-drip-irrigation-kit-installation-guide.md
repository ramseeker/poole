## Essential Shopping List for Your Raised Bed Drip Irrigation Kit

- **Drip Irrigation Kit** (Select based on bed size: e.g., 1/4" or 1/2" tubing system)
- **Pressure Regulator** (30–50 PSI, specific to your faucet type)
- **Backflow Preventer** (Required by most local codes)
- **Timer** (Battery-powered or Wi-Fi/automated)
- **Mainline Tubing** (1/2" polyethylene for long runs; 1/4" for branch lines)
- **Tee/Elbow Connectors** (1/2" and 1/4" sizes)
- **Drip Emitters** (Adjustable or fixed-flow, e.g., 0.5–2 GPH)
- **Drip Tape** (Optional, for row crops; 6–12" spacing)
- **Micro-Tubing** (1/4" for connecting emitters to plants)
- **Stakes** (For securing emitters/tape)
- **End Caps** (For sealing open tubing ends)
- **Hole Punch Tool** (For 1/4" fittings)
- **Tubing Cutter** (Clean cuts prevent leaks)
- **Filter** (100–150 mesh screen, to prevent clogging)
- **Faucet Adapter** (To connect kit to standard outdoor spigot)
- **Zip Ties or Clips** (For securing tubing to bed edges)

## Buying Guide: Why Each Item Matters

### **Drip Irrigation Kit**
- **Logic:** A pre-assembled kit (e.g., Rain Bird, Orbit, or DripWorks) bundles the core components—tubing, connectors, and emitters—saving time and ensuring compatibility. For a new homeowner, start with a kit rated for your bed’s square footage (e.g., 50–100 ft of 1/2" mainline). Avoid off-brand kits; they may lack pressure regulation or use brittle plastic.

### **Pressure Regulator**
- **Logic:** Most household water pressure (40–80 PSI) will burst or distort drip tubing. A regulator drops it to a safe 25–30 PSI. **Critical:** Match the regulator to your faucet type (hose thread vs. garden thread) and flow rate (GPM) of your system. A cheap regulator may fail within a season—invest in brass-bodied versions for durability.

### **Backflow Preventer**
- **Logic:** This prevents contaminated water (from soil or fertilizer) from siphoning back into your home’s drinking water. Most local plumbing codes require one. Use a vacuum breaker style (screws onto faucet). Never skip this—it’s a cheap insurance against health hazards.

### **Timer**
- **Logic:** Automates watering, crucial for new homeowners who may forget to turn off the hose. Choose a **battery-powered timer** (e.g., Orbit B-hyve) for simplicity, or a **Wi-Fi smart timer** (Rachio) if you want remote control via app. Key features: multiple start times, rain delay, and adjustable duration. Avoid mechanical timers—they drift over time.

### **Mainline Tubing (1/2")**
- **Logic:** Acts as the backbone delivering water to all emitters. Use **polyethylene** (UV-resistant, flexible) for longevity. Black tubing is standard (prevents algae growth inside). For beds longer than 50 ft, upgrade to 5/8" or 3/4" to reduce pressure loss. **Budget tip:** buy a 100-ft roll; leftover connects future beds.

### **Connectors (Tees, Elbows, End Caps)**
- **Logic:** Tees split water to multiple rows; elbows turn corners along bed edges. All must match tubing size (1/2" or 1/4"). Use **barbed fittings** (push-to-connect) for quick assembly—no glue. **Danger:** Cheap connectors crack in sun; choose UV-stabilized plastic or brass.

### **Drip Emitters**
- **Logic:** Deliver water directly to each plant’s root zone. **Adjustable (0–10 GPH)** are versatile for mixed crops—loosen for tomatoes, tighten for herbs. **Fixed-flow (0.5 GPH)** work best for uniform plants (e.g., lettuce rows). For sandy soil, use higher flow (1 GPH+); for clay, slower (0.5 GPH) prevents runoff. Avoid pre-punched emitter lines—they can’t be moved.

### **Drip Tape**
- **Logic:** Ideal for row crops (carrots, beans) where you need even watering along a line. Tape is flat, thin, and emits water through built-in holes every 6–12". **Caution:** Tape is fragile; bury it 1–2" under soil to protect from UV and accidental tears. Not reusable in future beds—buy per season.

### **Micro-Tubing (1/4")**
- **Logic:** Connects emitters from the mainline to specific plants. It’s flexible and can snake around obstacles. **Avoid kinking:** Use these only in short lengths (< 10 ft) to prevent flow reduction. Pair with **stakes** to hold emitter heads in place at each plant.

### **Stakes & Clips**
- **Logic:** Stakes secure emitter heads or spray stakes above soil level (prevents mud splash on leaves). Clips snap tubing to bed edges, reducing tripping hazards. Choose **metal stakes** for heavy soil; plastic ones flex and break in cold weather.

### **Hole Punch Tool**
- **Logic:** Creates precise holes for 1/4" fittings into 1/2" mainline. Using a knife or drill leads to leaks. A **ratcheting punch** ensures clean cuts without splitting tubing. **Non-negotiable:** Spend $5–10 on a dedicated tool.

### **Filter**
- **Logic:** Stops sand, rust, or debris from clogging emitters. Install between backflow preventer and timer. Use a **150-mesh screen** for municipal water; 100-mesh if you have well water (more sediment). A Y-filter with a clean-out valve simplifies maintenance.

### **Faucet Adapter**
- **Logic:** Standard spigots are 3/4" garden hose thread (GHT) or 1/2" pipe thread (NPT). Your kit likely uses 3/4" female GHT. A **brass adapter** prevents corrosion. If your spigot is recessed, get a **swivel elbow** adapter to connect easily.

### **Zip Ties**
- **Logic:** Attach mainline to raised bed frames or stakes against wind displacement. Use **UV-resistant** ties; standard ones become brittle in 6 months. Alternatively, use **snap-clips** that allow tubing to expand/contract with temperature.
