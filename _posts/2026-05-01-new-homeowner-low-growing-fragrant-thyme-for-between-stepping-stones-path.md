## Essential Shopping List for a Fragrant Thyme Stepping Stone Path

- **Plant:** 10-15 plugs of *Thymus serpyllum* 'Elfin' or 'Minimus' (low-growing, non-flowering variety)
- **Soil:** 1 bag of coarse sand (horticultural grade) + 1 bag of organic compost
- **Tools:** Hand trowel, small weeding knife, garden gloves
- **Mulch:** 1 bag of fine gravel or decomposed granite (2-5mm size)
- **Irrigation:** 1 soaker hose or a watering can with a fine rose
- **Optional:** Rooting hormone powder (for extra resilience)
- **Maintenance:** Organic slug bait (iron phosphate-based)

## Detailed Buying Guide

### **Selecting the Right Thyme Variety**
Not all thyme is created equal for path stepping stones. **Standard culinary thyme** (Thymus vulgaris) grows too tall and dense; it will smother cracks and become woody. Instead, look for:
- **Thymus serpyllum** (creeping thyme): This species stays under 3 inches (7.5 cm) tall and spreads horizontally. **'Elfin'** is the gold standard—it forms a dense, moss-like mat that tolerates foot traffic (rated for moderate walking). **'Minimus'** is even tighter and slower-growing, perfect for narrow gaps. **Why it matters:** Low-growing varieties won't block stone edges, and their shallow roots won't damage path stability.
- **Avoid** 'Woolly Thyme' (too fragile) and 'Lemon Thyme' (too tall, prone to leginess).
- **Purchase plugs or 2-inch pots**, not seeds. Seeds take 12+ months to establish; plugs give you an instant carpet.

### **Soil & Drainage Logic**
Thyme is a Mediterranean herb that demands **sharp drainage**. Standard garden soil or topsoil will cause root rot in damp climates.
- **Coarse sand** (horticultural grade, not play sand): Mix it 50:50 with your existing soil. The large particles create air pockets and prevent water pooling around roots. **Why it matters:** Path stones concentrate runoff; sand ensures water drains away within hours, not days.
- **Organic compost (optional):** Only add a handful if your soil is pure clay. Thyme prefers lean, low-nutrient conditions—too much compost leads to floppy, disease-prone growth.
- **Alternative:** Use a cactus/succulent potting mix (which has sand and perlite) if you're planting in a container-like path gap.

### **Tools for Precision Planting**
- **Hand trowel** (narrow blade): A 1.5-inch wide trowel lets you dig neat, shallow holes exactly between stones without widening the gap. A standard trowel is too bulky.
- **Small weeding knife** (e.g., Hori Hori): Essential for slicing through compacted soil under stones, removing grass roots that compete with thyme.
- **Garden gloves** (textured palms): Thyme stems are brittle; gloves prevent snapping while you press plants into place.

### **Mulch: Functional vs. Aesthetic**
- **Fine gravel (2-5mm):** Pea gravel or decomposed granite works as a top dressing around the thyme after planting. **Why it matters:** It suppresses weeds, keeps soil off thyme leaves (reduces rot), and reflects heat to warm the roots. **Avoid** large pebbles or bark mulch—bark holds moisture and rots, while large stones harm tender stems.
- **Layering:** Apply a 1/4-inch layer after planting, leaving the thyme's crown exposed.

### **Irrigation: Less Is More**
- **Soaker hose** (if the path is long and you're in a dry zone): Run it through the gaps the first 2 weeks to keep soil consistently moist. **Why it matters:** Thyme roots are shallow; erratic watering kills new transplants.
- **Watering can with a fine rose**: Best for small paths. Use room-temperature rainwater (avoids shock). **Overwatering is the #1 killer**—only water when the top 1/2 inch of soil feels dry.

### **Optional: Rooting Hormone to Beat Transplant Shock**
- **Powdered rooting hormone (IBA-based)**: Dip the plug's base before planting. **Why it matters:** Stimulates rapid root growth in the confined, high-stress environment between stones. Not strictly necessary, but it doubles success rates if your path is in full sun or windy.

### **Pest & Weed Prevention**
- **Organic slug bait** (iron phosphate, e.g., Sluggo): Slugs love thyme's tender new growth, especially in shady path gaps. **Why it matters:** Iron phosphate is non-toxic to pets and wildlife; conventional bait kills earthworms. Apply a small pinch around each plant every 2 weeks until the thyme matures (about 3 months).
