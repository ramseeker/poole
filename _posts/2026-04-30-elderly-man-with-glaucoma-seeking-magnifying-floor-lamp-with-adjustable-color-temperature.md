## Shopping List for an Elderly Man with Glaucoma: Magnifying Floor Lamp with Adjustable Color Temperature

- **Magnifying Floor Lamp** (with adjustable color temperature, 3x to 5x diopter, and a sturdy weighted base)
- **LED Bulb (or integrated LED)** with CRI ≥ 90, color temperature range 2700K–6500K
- **Articulated or Swivel Arm** for precise positioning over reading or task areas
- **Glare-Reduction Shade** (e.g., frosted lens, adjustable hood, or anti-glare coating)
- **Optional: Floor Stand with Casters** (if mobility is a concern for repositioning the lamp)
- **Optional: Prescription Reading Glasses** (dual-purpose for close-up work under the lamp)

## Buying Guide

### Why Adjustable Color Temperature Matters for Glaucoma

Glaucoma often causes reduced contrast sensitivity, glare sensitivity, and difficulty adapting to changes in light. **Adjustable color temperature** (from warm 2700K to cool 6500K) lets the user tailor the light to their specific needs and the time of day. **Cool light (4000K–6500K)** improves visual clarity and contrast for detailed tasks like reading or medication management, but can exacerbate glare. **Warm light (2700K–3200K)** reduces glare and is easier on aging eyes, especially during evening hours when blue light can disrupt sleep. **Choose a lamp with smooth, stepless dimming and color temperature control**, not just presets, for gradual transitions.

### Magnification Power: 3x to 5x Diopter

For an elderly user with glaucoma, **magnification helps compensate for reduced central vision** (common with advanced glaucoma). A **3x diopter** is ideal for reading standard print, while **5x diopter** suits fine detail work like sewing or labeling. **Avoid higher than 5x** for general use, as it narrows the field of view and requires very close positioning. **Opt for a lamp with a glass lens**—acrylic scratches more easily and can distort over time.

### Light Quality: High CRI and Low Glare

Glaucoma patients are highly sensitive to glare, which can cause pain or temporary vision loss. **Look for a CRI (Color Rendering Index) of 90 or above** to ensure colors are natural and not distorted—critical for distinguishing pill colors or reading fine print. **Integrated LED arrays** are preferable to replaceable bulbs because they are designed for even distribution. **Frosted or diffused lens covers** reduce harsh shadows and spread light evenly across the work area. **Avoid bare LED chips** or exposed bulbs.

### Adjustable Arm and Stable Base

The lamp must be **easily re-positionable** without the user bending or straining. A **fully articulated arm** with spring tension or locking joints lets the user angle the light downward at a comfortable 45-degree angle to minimize corneal reflection. **The base should be heavy or have a clamp mechanism** to prevent tipping. **Floor models should be at least 30–36 inches tall** (not counting the lamp head) to illuminate a standard chair or sofa arm height. **Check that the arm does not wobble** when extended fully—common in cheaper models.

### Additional Features: Dimming, Timer, and Memory

Glaucoma medications often cause dry eyes or light sensitivity. **Dimmable light** allows the user to reduce intensity for comfort while maintaining magnification. **A timer feature** (e.g., auto-off after 2–4 hours) prevents accidental overheating and saves energy. **Memory function** keeps the last settings when turned off/on, which is helpful for those with memory challenges. **Avoid touch-sensitive controls** if the user has tremor or arthritis; prefer physical buttons or a remote control.

### Safety and Ergonomics

- **Weighted base** (minimum 10 lbs) to prevent the lamp from tipping if bumped.
- **No sharp edges** on the arm or shade.
- **Cable management**: A cord that is at least 6 feet long and can be tucked away to reduce tripping risk.
- **Optional floor stand with locking casters**—useful if the user moves between rooms but cannot lift the lamp.
