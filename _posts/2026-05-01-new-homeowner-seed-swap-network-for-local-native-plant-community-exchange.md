## Essential Shopping List for a New Homeowner Native Plant Seed Swap Network

- **Seed envelopes** (recyclable/reusable, 100-pack minimum)
- **Seed storage containers** (airtight, UV-resistant, stackable)
- **Label maker** or waterproof markers
- **Seed identification cards** (pre-printed or blank templates)
- **Mesh bags** (for seed collection and sharing)
- **Fine-mesh sieve** (for seed cleaning)
- **Small craft funnel** (set of 2-3 sizes)
- **Desiccant packets** (silica gel, food-grade)
- **Digital scale** (grams, 0.1g precision)
- **Seed viability testing kit** (or paper towels + ziplock bags)
- **Community bulletin board** (physical or digital)
- **Seed library donation log** (ledger or spreadsheet template)

## Detailed Buying Guide: Why Each Item Matters

### **Seed Envelopes**
The backbone of any seed swap. Choose envelopes made from **recycled kraft paper** or **glassine**—they breathe slightly, preventing mold, yet keep seeds dry. Avoid plastic envelopes; they trap moisture. A batch of 100 ensures you can package seeds from 20-30 plant species in multiple portions. Fold-over flap designs seal without glue, easy for recipients to reuse.

### **Seed Storage Containers**
Seeds need stable, cool, dry conditions (think 40–50°F, 20–30% humidity). Use **airtight, UV-blocking glass or opaque plastic** containers. Stackable, modular bins work best for categorizing by plant family (e.g., pollinator flowers, grasses, shrubs). Pro tip: Label the container, not just the envelope inside, to avoid accidental seed mixing when digging through the bin at a swap.

### **Label Maker or Waterproof Markers**
Misidentified seeds are the #1 cause of swap network failures. A **thermal label maker** (e.g., Brother P-Touch) prints durable, smudge-proof labels perfect for envelopes and bins. If budget-conscious, use **oil-based Sharpie markers**—water-based ones fade with humidity. Always include: common name, scientific name, collection date, and a brief growth note (e.g., "sun-loving, blooms June–Aug").

### **Seed Identification Cards**
Create a standardized card for each seed packet that includes: habitat requirements, planting depth, light needs, and a photo of the mature plant. Pre-print blank templates and leave space for the collector/parent seed donor to fill in. This eliminates guesswork for new homeowners who may not recognize seedlings versus weeds. Laminate cards for reuse.

### **Mesh Bags**
Essential for gathering seeds in the wild or from your own garden without damage. Use **fine-mesh nylon or cotton drawstring bags** (e.g., paint strainer bags or tea bags). Avoid closed plastic bags—they trap moisture and cause seeds to rot. Mesh allows airflow during transport and while drying. Keep separate color bags for different plant species to avoid cross-contamination.

### **Fine-Mesh Sieve**
Clean seeds from chaff, dirt, and mold spores before swapping. A **stainless steel, 10–20 mesh sieve** catches seeds of the 1–3mm size typical of native wildflowers. Rinse between uses to prevent disease spread. Pair with a shallow bowl for sifting. Do not skip this—dirty seeds fail germination tests and damage network reputation.

### **Small Craft Funnel Set**
Portion seeds into equal volumes for swaps without spills. Use **stainless steel or BPA-free plastic funnels** with 1/4-inch, 1/2-inch, and 1-inch openings. The smallest works for poppy or milkweed seeds; the largest for acorns or larger tree seeds. Label each funnel with its intended seed size range to avoid confusion.

### **Desiccant Packets**
Moisture is the silent killer of seed viability. **Silica gel packs** (food-grade, indicator type that change color when saturated) keep storage containers at <30% relative humidity. Recharge them in a 200°F oven for 2 hours when they turn pink. Use 2–3 packets per gallon-sized container. Avoid rice or salt—they attract pests.

### **Digital Scale**
Seed counts vary; volume is unreliable for small seeds. A **pocket digital scale with 0.1g precision** ensures fairness: each member contributes equal seed weight (e.g., 5g per species). Calibrate monthly with standard weights. Inconsistent portions erode trust in the network. Include tare function to subtract envelope weight.

### **Seed Viability Testing Kit**
Before swapping, test random samples from each batch. The **standard method**: place 10 seeds on a damp paper towel in a ziplock bag, keep at 70°F, count germination after 7–14 days. If <60% germinate, discard or label "low viability." A dedicated testing kit (paper towels, ziplocks, spray bottle) keeps hobbyists organized. Share results with the network to track parent stock quality.

### **Community Bulletin Board (Physical or Digital)**
Centralize swap logistics—who has what, when, and how to trade. For low-tech, a **cork or magnetic board** in a common area (community center, library porch) works. For remote suburbs, use a **free platform** like Google Sheets or a dedicated Facebook group. Include: a "wish list" column, "available" column, and a last-traded date. Without this, swaps devolve into chaos.

### **Seed Library Donation Log**
Track who gives and receives—essential for network sustainability. Use a **pre-printed ledger book** or a **Google Form** integrated into your bulletin board. Log fields: date, member name, species donated, weight/quantity, species received. Review quarterly to identify gaps (e.g., overabundance of one species, lack of shade-tolerant plants). This data also helps you apply for local gardening grants.
