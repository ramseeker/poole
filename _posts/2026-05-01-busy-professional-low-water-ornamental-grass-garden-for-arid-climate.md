## Essential Shopping List

- **Ornamental grasses (clumping, drought-tolerant):** Blue fescue (*Festuca glauca*), Mexican feathergrass (*Nassella tenuissima*), Pink muhly (*Muhlenbergia capillaris*), Blue grama (*Bouteloua gracilis*).
- **Agave or yucca specimens** (2-3 focal points): Agave parryi, Yucca glauca.
- **Hardscape surface** (gravel or decomposed granite): 3/8" washed gravel or stabilized decomposed granite, enough for 2-3" depth over planned area.
- **Professional-grade drip irrigation kit** (timer + drip line): Battery-operated or Wi-Fi smart timer with 1/4" soaker dripline.
- **Weed barrier fabric** (heavy-duty, permeable): 6-oz non-woven geotextile.
- **Soil amendment:** Coarse sand or pumice (for existing clay), organic compost (1 part per 5 parts native soil).
- **Hand tools:** Ergonomic soil knife (Hori Hori), narrow trowel, long-handled pruners.
- **Mulch:** 2" of smooth river pebbles or heat-reflective light-colored gravel.
- **Slow-release fertilizer:** Low-nitrogen formula (5-10-10 or similar) for grasses.

## Detailed Buying Guide

### Ornamental Grasses and Accent Plants
The **core of your garden** lies in selecting grasses that thrive on infrequent water and require only one major cleanup per year (late winter). **Blue fescue** forms perfect, compact blue mounds that don’t spread; ideal for edges. **Mexican feathergrass** adds wispy, wind-moved texture with almost zero maintenance. **Pink muhly** provides a dramatic, cloud-like pink bloom in fall, a knockout reward for minimal effort. For structure, choose **agave or yucca**—these succulents act as architectural punctuation, needing water only when leaves begin to pucker. **Avoid** running grasses like bamboo or ribbon grass; they require constant division and may overtake the bed.

### Hardscape Surface (Gravel or Decomposed Granite)
In an arid climate, bare soil between plants wastes water to evaporation and invites weeds. A 2-3" layer of **3/8" washed gravel** or **stabilized decomposed granite** (mixed with a binder) suppresses weeds instantly, reflects heat away from roots, and creates a clean, professional look that never needs mowing. **Washed gravel** drains fast, preventing root rot, while decomposed granite compacts nicely for paths but requires re-edging. For a low-touch option, choose larger angular gravel (like ¾" crushed) that won’t scatter in wind.

### Professional-Grade Drip Irrigation
Manual watering is not sustainable for a busy professional. Invest in a **smart drip timer** that connects to your phone (e.g., Rain Bird ST8I-WIFI or Orbit B-hyve). Set it to water deeply once every 14-21 days after initial establishment. Use **1/4" drip tubing** with emitters spaced 12-18 inches apart, or a soaker hose laid in zig-zags under the gravel. The logic: deep, infrequent water trains grass roots to grow deep, making them truly drought-tolerant. Avoid spray heads—they lose 30% water to evaporation in arid heat.

### Weed Barrier Fabric
Skip cheap black landscape fabric from big-box stores; it degrades in sun and traps moisture. Choose **heavy-duty non-woven geotextile** (6 oz or higher). This fabric allows air and water to pass through while blocking light. Lay it under your gravel, cutting X-slits for each plant. This prevents weed seeds from germinating in the gravel and eliminates the need for manual weeding for 5+ years.

### Soil Amendment
Most arid-climate soils are either heavy clay (which holds too much water) or pure sand (which drains too fast). For clay soil, add **30% coarse sand or pumice** to the planting hole to improve drainage—grasses hate wet feet. For sandy soil, mix in **a small amount of organic compost** (no more than 15%) to retain just enough moisture for root establishment. **Do not** enrich the entire bed; grasses are adapted to lean soil.

### Hand Tools
You need tools that reduce physical effort. The **Hori Hori soil knife** is the best multipurpose tool for planting grasses: it cuts fabric, digs narrow holes, and measures depth. A **narrow trowel** (with inch markings) prevents oversized planting holes that waste water. **Long-handled pruners** (like Felco 2) allow you to shear back dead grass stalks in winter without bending over—critical for maintaining the garden’s clean look with minimal back strain.

### Mulch (River Pebbles or Light Gravel)
Above the weed barrier, add **smooth river pebbles** or **light-colored gravel** (tan, white, or decomposed granite). Light colors reflect sunlight, keeping root zones up to 10°F cooler than dark mulch. Smooth stones won’t harm grass blades when they sway. Avoid bark mulch—it retains moisture, hosts pests, and decomposes quickly in arid heat, requiring annual replacement.

### Slow-Release Fertilizer
Grasses and agaves are adapted to low nutrients. Use a **low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus/potassium** fertilizer (like a 5-10-10 or Espoma Bulb-tone). **Apply once** in early spring at half the label rate. The logic: excess nitrogen produces lush, floppy growth that requires staking and watering—exactly what you don’t want. Phosphorus supports root depth and bloom strength in muhly grass; potassium boosts drought resistance.
