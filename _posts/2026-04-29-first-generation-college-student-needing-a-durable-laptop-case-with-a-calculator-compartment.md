## Shopping List: First-Gen College Student Laptop Case with Calculator Compartment

- **Laptop Case** (sleeve or bag) with dedicated calculator slot
- **Scientific calculator** (e.g., Texas Instruments TI-84 Plus CE)
- **Screen protector** for laptop (anti-glare)
- **Laptop charging brick** (original or compatible)
- **USB-C hub** (multi-port)
- **Pencil pouch** (for stylus, pens, highlighters)
- **Wet wipes or microfibre cloth** (cleaning case/calculator)
- **Laptop lock** (cable style, for library/study rooms)
- **Cable organizer** (Velcro straps or small zippered pouch)

## Buying Guide

### 1. Laptop Case with Calculator Compartment: The Core Item
**Logic:** As a first-gen student, you need to protect a major investment while avoiding buying two separate bags. A case with a dedicated calculator compartment eliminates the risk of the calculator scratching your screen or getting lost in a backpack pocket.  
**What to look for:**
- **Padding:** Minimum 1-centimeter foam or memory foam on all sides. Look for “shock-absorbing” lining (e.g., neoprene or thick synthetic felt).
- **Calculator slot dimensions:** Measure your calculator (most TI-84s are about 7.5 x 4 x 0.5 inches). The slot should be snug, not loose, with its own zipper or elastic strap.
- **Material:** Water-resistant nylon or polyester (avoid cheap canvas that absorbs spills). A dark color hides wear better.
- **Closure:** Double zippers are ideal—they prevent the case from gaping open if overloaded.  
*Tip: Avoid cheap “sleeve-only” cases that lack calculator pockets. A front pouch or side pocket is non-negotiable.*

### 2. Scientific Calculator: The Academic Lifeline
**Logic:** Engineering, math, chemistry, physics—all require a non-CAS (Computer Algebra System) calculator for exams. First-gen students often can’t borrow one last-minute.  
- **Model:** TI-84 Plus CE (color) is standard; TI-36X Pro is cheaper and allowed on most exams.  
- **Protection:** Store it in the case’s calculator compartment *only*. The compartment acts as a protective sleeve against key jamming.  
*Watch out for: Rechargeable vs. battery-powered. Batteries (AAA) are safer for all-night study sessions—plug-in calculators die mid-exam.*

### 3. Screen Protector (Anti-Glare)
**Logic:** Laptop screens get greasy fingerprints from calculator use. An anti-glare protector reduces eye strain and hides smudges.  
- **Type:** Hardened tempered glass (0.3mm) for scratch resistance, not plastic film.  
- **Installation:** Buy a kit with a hinge guide (easy alignment) to avoid bubbles.

### 4. Laptop Charging Brick (Original or Third-Party)
**Logic:** Don’t let a frayed cord ruin a study session. First-gen budgets often rely on used laptops—verify voltage (19-20V for most).  
- **Recommendation:** Buy a spare for your backpack case (keep the original at your desk). Look for GaN (Gallium Nitride) bricks—they’re 60% smaller and run cooler.  
*Caveat: Avoid cheap $10 chargers—explosions are rare but real. Opt for reputable brands like Anker or laptop OEM.*

### 5. USB-C Hub (Multi-Port)
**Logic:** Modern laptops ditch USB-A and HDMI ports. You’ll need them for group projects (projector hookup) or thumb drives from the library.  
- **Essentials:** HDMI, 3 USB-A, SD card slot, USB-C pass-through (for charging).  
- **Size:** Choose a hub that fits in the same front pocket as your calculator, not a separate bag.

### 6. Pencil Pouch (Thin Profile)
**Logic:** Your case likely has limited internal organization. A slim pencil pouch keeps pens, highlighters, and a stylus from scratching your laptop.  
- **Material:** Mesh (ventilated) or clear PVC (quick visibility).  
- **What to store:** 2 mechanical pencils, 1 erasable pen, 1 highlighter, 1 stylus (for touchscreens).

### 7. Wet Wipes or Microfibre Cloth
**Logic:** Calculator buttons and laptop screens get greasy. Hand sanitizer or wet wipes (alcohol-free, to avoid screen damage) keep both clean.  
- **Storage:** Tuck a single microfibre cloth into the case’s external zippered pocket. Avoid wet wipes inside the case (moisture risks).

### 8. Cable Organizer
**Logic:** Loose cables create tangles and wear out charging ports. A simple Velcro strap or small zippered pouch prevents this.  
- **Tip:** Use a 6-inch strap for the charging brick cable, and a separate small pouch for the USB hub cable.

### 9. Laptop Lock (Cable Style)
**Logic:** First-gen students may be the first to claim a library desk. A lock prevents theft when you step away to ask for help.  
- **Compatibility:** Check that your laptop has a Kensington security slot (most do). Buy a lock with a coiled cable (retractable, less bulky).  
*Cost: $8-$15. Cheaper than replacing a laptop.*

### 10. Backup Storage (Optional but Wise)
**Logic:** Calculator work often spills over into spreadsheets. A 64GB USB-C flash drive fits in any pocket and saves you from cloud-only reliance (if Wi-Fi drops).  
- **Recommendation:** Samsung FIT Plus or SanDisk Ultra Fit—they’re micro-size and won’t strain the port.

---

**Final Pro-Tip:** Test your case’s calculator compartment *before* your first class. Slide the calculator in and out while the laptop is inside. If it’s tight, the zipper will snag fabric or the calculator’s corners may tear the sleeve. If it’s loose, use a thin piece of sticky-back foam to fill the gap. This $1 fix saves you from a broken case during midterms.
