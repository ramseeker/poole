## Essential Shopping List

- **Wall-Mounted Tool Rack** (e.g., 12-slot plastic/metal holder)
- **Magnetic Tool Strip** (18-inch or longer)
- **Heavy-Duty Shovel Hooks** (2-pack or 4-pack)
- **Bungee Cords or Retractable Tie-Down Straps** (assorted lengths)
- **Small Shelf Unit** (wire or plastic, 12 x 30 inches)
- **Clear Plastic Bins** (stackable, medium-sized, 10-15 quart)
- **Label Maker or Permanent Markers** (waterproof ink)
- **Corner Mounted Hose Guide or Hanger** (if storing a coil hose)
- **Quick-Release Pegboard Panel** (optional, for small hand tools)
- **Zip Ties (reusable)** or **Cable Clips** (for cord management)

## ## Buying Guide: Logic for Each Item

**Wall-Mounted Tool Rack**  
The core of any compact shed is vertical storage. A rack with individual slots prevents rakes, hoes, and leaf blowers from tangling into a mess. Look for models with adjustable or open-ended slots—these let you slide long handles in and out without lifting the entire tool. Plastic racks resist rust, but metal racks with a powder coating can support heavier items like post-hole diggers. Mount it at least 18 inches from the ceiling to avoid hitting your head.

**Magnetic Tool Strip**  
Trowels, pruners, and small clippers vanish into shed corners. A magnetic strip (often used for knives in kitchens) mounted on a wall or door instantly organizes metal hand tools. It keeps them visible and dry, reducing rust. Choose a strip with at least 15 pounds of pull force—this handles heavy shears. The benefit? You never dig through a bin for a specific tool again.

**Heavy-Duty Shovel Hooks**  
Standard hooks sometimes slide off with a long-handled shovel’s weight. Dedicated shovel hooks (often with a rubber coating or a deep, curved cradle) secure flat-blade tools vertically. Mount them in pairs to balance weight. This prevents shovels from falling and damaging other equipment. For wheelbarrows, use two hooks side-by-side to hang the handles flat.

**Bungee Cords or Retractable Tie-Down Straps**  
Long, thin items like extension cords or garden hoses become a tangled nightmare. Loop a bungee cord around a hook or peg, then wrap the hose/cord around the shed’s wall cleats. The bungee keeps it coiled tight. A retractable strap with a built-in release allows quick access. This is cheaper than buying a dedicated hose reel.

**Small Shelf Unit**  
A deep shed often wastes cubic feet on the floor. A wall-mounted or freestanding shelf (12 inches deep, 30 inches wide) in a corner stores bulky items like fertilizer bags or watering cans. Choose wire shelving for airflow—this prevents mold on damp soil bags. Adjustable shelves let you store a tall spray bottle next to a flat box of gloves.

**Clear Plastic Bins**  
Every small shed needs “dump zones” for things like plant markers, gloves, nozzles, and seed packets. Clear bins let you see contents without opening each box. Stackable designs maximize vertical space. Buy bins with locking lids if your shed gets humid—it seals out pests. Use one bin per category: “Seasonal Tools,” “Gardening Chemicals,” “Repair Supplies.”

**Label Maker or Permanent Markers**  
Without labels, bins become chaos within a month. Waterproof markers or a simple label maker (refillable tape) ensures you know where “Hedge Trimmer Attachments” lives. Write on matte tape, not glossy—it stays legible longer. For a budget option, use painter’s tape and a sharpie; replace tape yearly.

**Corner Mounted Hose Guide or Hanger**  
If you keep a short garden hose in your shed (50 feet or less), a wall-mounted hanger saves floor space. Corner mounts angle the hose away from walls, preventing kinks. Avoid cheap plastic models—they snap under winter frost. Use a rubber-coated metal guide. For longer hoses, consider a fast-draining reel mounted to a stud.

**Quick-Release Pegboard Panel (optional)**  
A small pegboard (2x2 feet) frees a section of wall for 50+ small items: screwdrivers, clippers, nozzles, and brushes. Quick-release panels are easier to install than traditional pegboard (no furring strips). Use pegboard hooks with stoppers—they hold items during wind or bumps. Limit to one panel; too much pegboard overwhelms a tiny shed.

**Zip Ties (reusable) or Cable Clips**  
Tidy electrical cords, string lines, or battery chargers with a single zip tie. Reusable ties (with a release tab) let you undo and re-coil without cutting. Attach them to the inside of the shed door for chargers—this keeps them off the floor. Cable clips (stick-on or screw-in) route power tool cords along the wall so they don’t trip you.

**Final Layout Tip:** Mount the tool rack on the door or a side wall (not behind the wheelbarrow). Place the shelf opposite the door for easy access. Label all bins before the first tool goes in. A compact shed organized this way requires less than 15 minutes to tidy, even after heavy use.
