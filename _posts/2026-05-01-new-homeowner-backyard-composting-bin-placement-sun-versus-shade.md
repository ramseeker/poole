## Essential Shopping List for Backyard Composting (New Homeowner Edition)

- **Compost Bin** (choose one: tumbling bin, stationary bin, or DIY options)
- **Compost Thermometer** (long-stem, 20-inch probe)
- **Garden Fork** or **Aerator Tool**
- **Moisture Meter** (optional but helpful)
- **Carbon-Nitrogen Mix Materials** (browns: dried leaves, cardboard; greens: grass clippings, kitchen scraps)
- **Burlap Sacks** or **Shade Cloth** (for sun-site mitigation)
- **Watering Can** with rose attachment
- **Hardware Cloth** (1/4-inch mesh, for rodent-proofing base)
- **Compost Activator** (optional, for speed)

## Detailed Buying Guide

### ## Compost Bin: Sun vs. Shade Placement Logic

Your bin choice directly interacts with sun or shade. **If you place the bin in full sun** (6+ hours daily), consider:
- **Tumbling bins** (e.g., compost tumblers on a stand) – These heat up fast in sun, accelerating decomposition, but can dry out. Look for **dark-colored, UV-stabilized plastic** (avoids cracking) and a **dual-chamber design** (allows one side to "cook" while you fill the other).
- **Stationary plastic bins** (e.g., open-bottom cone styles) – Sun-heated models work well but require **ventilation slots** to prevent overheating. Avoid thin, cheap plastic that becomes brittle.
- **DIY wooden bins** (3-bin system) – Ideal in sun if made from **cedar or cypress** (rot-resistant). Build slatted sides for airflow. Avoid pressure-treated lumber (chemicals leach).

**If you place the bin in full or partial shade** (under a tree, north side of house):
- **Worm bins** (vermicomposting) – Thrive in cool, dark shade. Use a **stackable tray system** with a spigot for liquid collection. Essential: **moisture-retaining bedding** (shredded newspaper, coconut coir).
- **Closed tumbling bins** – Shade slows decomposition, so choose a **batch-style tumbling bin** (holds 10-15 gallons) that you can manually turn daily to compensate for lower heat.
- **Stationary bins with insulated walls** – Look for **double-walled plastic** or **foam-core** models. Shade requires more insulation to retain microbial heat.

**Pro tip**: For new homeowners, **start with a stationary bin in partial shade** (morning sun, afternoon shade). This balances temperature, moisture, and ease of access while you learn.

### ## Compost Thermometer: Non-Negotiable for Sun Placement

A **20-inch stainless steel probe thermometer** is critical if your bin gets sun. Sun exposure can spike internal temperatures to 160°F+, killing beneficial microbes and slowing decomposition. Logic:
- **Why you need it**: You must maintain 130-150°F for hot composting (sun) or 90-110°F for cool composting (shade). A thermometer prevents burnout or under-heating.
- **Features**: Choose a **dial face that’s easy to read in bright sun** (white background) or a **digital model with a backlight** for shade conditions. Avoid cheap glass thermometers (break easily).

### ## Garden Fork or Aerator Tool

**Sun-scorched compost** becomes compacted and dry; **shade-moist compost** can become anaerobic (soggy, smelly). A **sturdy garden fork** (4-5 tines, 48-inch handle) lets you turn the pile weekly. For tumbling bins, a **crank-style aerator** (long rod with folding tines) is better.

### ## Moisture Meter: Sun vs. Shade Criticality

**Sun placement** dries out compost rapidly. A **two-pronged moisture meter** (0-10 scale) prevents you from over-watering (leaching nutrients) or under-watering (halting decomposition). **Aim for 4-6** (like a wrung-out sponge).  
**Shade placement** retains moisture longer. A meter here prevents the “swamp” problem. Get a **digital model with a large screen** (easier in low light). Skip cheap analog meters (inaccurate).

### ## Carbon-Nitrogen Mix Materials: Sun vs. Shade Adjustments

**Sun-located bins** need **extra browns** (carbon-rich: dried leaves, wood chips, cardboard) because sun accelerates nitrogen loss. Store browns in **ventilated bins** (e.g., galvanized trash can with holes) near your bin.  
**Shade-located bins** need **extra greens** (nitrogen-rich: grass clippings, vegetable scraps) to overcome slower microbial activity. Keep a **countertop mini-bin** for daily kitchen scraps, plus a **sealed bucket** for grass clippings (prevents smells in shade).

### ## Burlap Sacks or Shade Cloth (Sun Placement Only)

If your bin sits in unrelenting sun, **burlap sacks** (draped over the bin) or a **40-50% shade cloth** (cut to size, secured with bungee cords) reduce surface temperature by 10-20°F while preserving airflow. Why not tarp? Tarps trap heat and moisture, creating anaerobic conditions.

### ## Watering Can with Rose Attachment

Sun-exposed bins need **gentle, even watering** daily. A **2-gallon watering can with a fine rose** (shower head) prevents soil compaction. For shade bins, use a **1-gallon can** (less water needed) every 3-4 days. Avoid hose sprayers (over-wets).

### ## Hardware Cloth (Rodent-Proofing)

**Critical for both sun and shade** – rodents love warm compost. Buy a **1/4-inch galvanized hardware cloth** roll (not 1/2-inch – mice squeeze through). Cut a circle to fit under the bin’s base, plus rectangular pieces to line any vent holes. For bins on concrete, skip this.

### ## Compost Activator (Optional but Smart)

In **sun**, activators (blood meal, alfalfa meal) speed up the “burn” rate. In **shade**, they introduce microbes that struggle in cool conditions. Buy a **dry, organic granular activator** – avoid liquid versions (wash away in rain). Use sparingly; over-applying causes ammonia odors.

### Final Placement Recommendation

**Compromise**: Place your bin in a spot with **morning sun and afternoon shade** (e.g., east-facing fence line). This gives you the heat of sun for decomposition without the drying/overheating risks. If your yard offers only one extreme:
- **Full sun**: Choose a **tumbling bin** (better moisture control) and **burden with burlap**.
- **Deep shade**: Go for a **worm bin** or **insulated stationary bin** and add more “greens” weekly.
