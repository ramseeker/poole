## Essential Fall Cleanup Shopping List for New Homeowners (No Tarp Needed)

- **Leaf Scoops** (pair)
- **Leaf Rake** (bow rake or ergonomic leaf rake)
- **Leaf Blower** (corded or battery-powered)
- **Electric or Reel Mower** (mulching option)
- **Lawn Bags** (paper composting bags or heavy-duty plastic bin bags)
- **Garden Gloves** (textured, waterproof)
- **Knee Pad or Stool** (for seated cleanup)
- **Wheelbarrow or Garden Cart** (if yard is larger than 1,000 sq ft)

---

## Detailed Buying Guide: Logic Behind Each Item

### Leaf Scoops (Pair)
**Why you need them instead of a tarp:**  
A tarp requires multiple trips, shaking leaves into a pile, and usually a second person. Leaf scoops turn your hands into oversized claws, allowing you to pick up massive volumes of leaves directly into a bag or wheelbarrow. They also prevent you from bending over repeatedly—a lifesaver for lower back strain.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Ergonomic handles** (curved or padded) to reduce wrist fatigue.  
- **Material:** Hard plastic (polyethylene) works best; avoid brittle, thin plastic that cracks in cold weather.  
- **Pair with matching clips** (some models snap together for one-handed grab-and-dump operations).

### Leaf Rake (Bow Rake or Ergonomic Leaf Rake)
**Why you need it instead of a blower-only approach:**  
A rake reaches under bushes, into tight corners, and against flower beds where a blower blows debris into hard-to-reach spots. A bow rake (curved tines) is ideal for loosening wet, matted leaves on grass without digging into the turf.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Adjustable handle length** (fits your height to prevent hunching).  
- **Fan-shaped tines** (spread widely for covering more ground quickly).  
- **Weight:** Aluminum or fiberglass handles are lighter than steel and won’t rust.  
- **Optional: Mulching rake** - a rake with serrated edges that shred leaves into smaller pieces for faster composting.

### Leaf Blower (Corded or Battery-Powered)
**Why you need it (without a tarp):**  
A blower moves leaves from hard-to-rake areas (driveways, patios, under decks) into one central pile that you can then scoop. You don’t need a giant gas-powered model; a lightweight corded electric (cheaper, continuous power) or a battery-powered one (portable, quiet) works fine for most new homeowner yards (under 0.25 acres).  
**What to look for:**  
- **Airflow (CFM) over speed (mph):** Look for 300–500 CFM. Higher CFM moves wet, heavy leaves.  
- **Variable speed trigger** (prevents blowing leaves into neighbors’ yards).  
- **Bag attachment?** Skip it—you’re using scoops. A flat nozzle is better for channeling leaves into a pile.  
- **Corded:** Clips to an outdoor extension cord (14-gauge minimum).  
- **Battery:** Requires a 40V–56V system; check runtime—30 minutes is minimum for a small yard.

### Electric or Reel Mower (Mulching Option)
**Why you need it (without a tarp):**  
You’re not using a tarp, so you’ll want to minimize leaf volume. Running a mulching mower over leaves (when dry) shreds them into tiny bits that decompose directly into the lawn, adding nitrogen. This reduces the amount you need to scoop by 60–80%. A reel mower is quiet and zero-emissions; a cordless electric mulching mower is faster.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Mulching blade** (curved, with a closed deck to recirculate leaves).  
- **Grass bag** not required for mulching—just mow without catching.  
- **Reel mower:** Ideal for small lawns (<3,000 sq ft) and thin leaf cover. Manual, but quiet and cheap.  
- **Electric mower:** For thicker leaf layers; look for a “mulch” setting that blocks the side discharge.

### Lawn Bags (Paper Composting or Heavy-Duty Plastic)
**Why you need them (without a tarp):**  
Without a tarp to heap leaves onto, you’ll transfer directly into bags. Paper bags are compostable and accepted by most municipal leaf pickup programs (check your city’s rules). Heavy-duty plastic bags are sturdier for wet leaves but require landfill disposal.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Size:** 30–33 gallon is standard—fits leaf scoops without overflowing.  
- **Paper bags:** Choose corrugated sides (stronger when wet) and a flat bottom for standing upright.  
- **Plastic bags:** Pick 2–3 mil thickness, dark green or clear (bags with “compostable” labels disintegrate in wet conditions).  
- **Avoid leaf bags that require a stand**—you’ll be using the scoops to hold the bag open.

### Garden Gloves (Textured, Waterproof)
**Why you need them:**  
Leaf scoops are plastic, but wet leaves and dirt will soak your hands. Non-textured gloves cause slipped scoops and messy wrists. Waterproof gloves with rubberized palms give you grip on wet grass and protect against thorns (if you’re raking near rose bushes or invasive weeds).  
**What to look for:**  
- **Material:** Nitrile-coated or latex-dipped for waterproofing.  
- **Cuff length:** At least 4 inches above the wrist to keep debris out.  
- **Breathable back:** Mesh or cotton panels prevent sweating in mild weather.

### Knee Pad or Stool
**Why you need it:**  
Raking and scooping force you to stoop or kneel—especially when reaching under low hanging branches or picking up leaves from ground level. A simple foam knee pad ($10–15) saves your knees. A foldable garden stool (around 18 inches tall) allows you to sit while scooping, reducing back strain.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Knee pad:** Closed-cell foam (won’t absorb water).  
- **Stool:** Look for a frame with non-slip feet and a capacity of 250+ lbs.

### Wheelbarrow or Garden Cart (If Yard >1,000 sq ft)
**Why you need it (without a tarp):**  
Without a tarp to drag leaves to a central pile, you’ll need to carry bags. A wheelbarrow lets you move multiple full bags at once, or you can dump leaves directly into the wheelbarrow from your leaf scoops. A garden cart (two or four wheels) is more stable than a wheelbarrow on slopes.  
**What to look for:**  
- **Capacity:** 6 cubic feet minimum for a small yard; 10+ for larger areas.  
- **Material:** Steel or polypropylene. Poly won’t rust and is lighter.  
- **Tires:** Pneumatic (air-filled) for bumpy yards; flat-free foam for puncture resistance.  
- **Pro tip:** Use the wheelbarrow *without* bags initially—scoop leaves into it, then dump into a designated compost pile or curb side.

**Final tip:** Skip the tarp entirely by creating a dedicated “leaf drop zone” (e.g., a corner of the yard or a compost bin) where you wheelbarrow all debris. This saves you from having to spread, fold, or shake a wet tarp.
