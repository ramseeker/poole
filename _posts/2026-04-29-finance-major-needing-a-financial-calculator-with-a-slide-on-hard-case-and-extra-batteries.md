## Shopping List: Essential Items for a Finance Major

- **Primary Financial Calculator** (e.g., HP 12C, Texas Instruments BA II Plus Professional)
- **Custom Slide-On Hard Case** (specifically designed for your calculator model)
- **Spare Battery Pack** (CR2032 lithium coin cell for TI models; CR2032 or button cell for HP)
- **Battery Carrier/Storage Case** (small, hard-shell case to hold spare batteries)
- **Optional: Mechanical Keyboard Cover** (for key protection if using in a messy backpack)

---

## Buying Guide

### 1. Financial Calculator

**Why you need it:** Financial calculators are not just for exams. They are your daily toolkit for time-value-of-money (TVM) calculations, cash flow analysis, net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), and amortization schedules. A standard scientific calculator lacks these dedicated functions.

**What to look for:**
- **Model:** The **Texas Instruments BA II Plus Professional** (or the standard **BA II Plus**) is the gold standard in university finance programs. It handles TVM, cash flows, and statistics. Alternatively, the **HP 12C** is legendary for its Reverse Polish Notation (RPN) and durability, but has a steeper learning curve.
- **Professional vs. Standard:** The “Professional” version of the BA II Plus adds a **net future value (NFV)** function, modified duration, and more memory—essential for advanced corporate finance courses.
- **Key feel:** Choose a model with tactile, clicky keys that won’t slip under pressure. The BA II Plus has a rubber membrane; the HP 12C has hard plastic keys.
- **Display:** Look for a high-contrast LCD (some HP models offer a backlit display for low-light study sessions).

**Pro tip:** *If your program uses Excel for finance, the BA II Plus’s keystrokes align better with spreadsheet functions. The HP 12C is better for professional certification exams (CFA).*

---

### 2. Slide-On Hard Case

**Why you need it:** The calculator’s included soft pouch or slipcover provides minimal protection. A **slide-on hard case** is a rigid, clamshell-style cover that snaps onto the calculator, protecting the screen, buttons, and body from pressure, scratches, and drops in a backpack filled with textbooks, pens, or laptops.

**What to look for:**
- **Custom Fit:** Ignore universal cases. A case designed for your exact model (e.g., “Texas Instruments BA II Plus Hard Case”) will have cutouts for the screen and backlight button, and a snug fit that prevents the calculator from rattling.
- **Material:** Choose **polypropylene** or **polycarbonate**. These are lightweight, shatter-resistant, and can withstand being sat on. Avoid cheap acrylic—it cracks easily.
- **Closure:** Look for a case with a **locking tab or button** (not just friction fit). This prevents the case from sliding off when you grab it by the cover.
- **Thickness:** A case that adds only 2–4mm to the calculator is ideal. It should slide easily into a pocket or bag slot.

**Pro tip:** *Some brands like “CalcPocket” or “KeebCase” offer cases with a built-in battery compartment cover window, so you can check your spare battery status without opening the calculator.*

---

### 3. Spare Battery Pack

**Why you need it:** Financial calculators use small coin cells (usually **CR2032** for the BA II Plus Professional). If your calculator dies during an exam—or worse, during a timed case study competition—you lose all unsaved work. A fresh spare battery eliminates panic.

**What to look for:**
- **Type:** Confirm the exact battery model. The BA II Plus Professional uses one **CR2032 3V lithium coin cell**. The HP 12C uses either a CR2032 or a smaller button cell (check your model).
- **Brand:** Stick to **Energizer** or **Duracell**. Avoid generic store brands—they have higher self-discharge rates and may leak after a year.
- **Quantity:** Buy a **4-pack** or **6-pack**. One goes in the calculator, one in your storage case, and the rest are backups for finals season.
- **Voltage Rating:** Ensure it’s **3V**. Using a 1.5V battery will cause erratic behavior.

**Pro tip:** *Write the purchase date on each battery with a marker. Replace the working battery every 12–18 months even if it still “works,” as voltage drop can cause calculation errors.*

---

### 4. Battery Carrier/Storage Case

**Why you need it:** Loose coin cells in a bag are a hazard. They can short-circuit against keys or coins, causing heat or even fire. A small, hard case keeps them safe and organized.

**What to look for:**
- **Size:** Look for a **compact pill fob** (like a mini Altoids tin) or a **keychain battery holder**. The holder should be slightly larger than the battery itself to prevent rattling.
- **Material:** **Metal tins** are ideal (anti-static, crush-resistant). Plastic cases work but can crack under pressure.
- **Capacity:** A case that holds **2–4 batteries** is sufficient. Don’t over-stuff—if batteries rub together, they can discharge.
- **Labeling:** If you use different battery models (e.g., for your calculator and a remote), choose a case with a clear window or label area.

**Pro tip:** *Peel the thin plastic film off new batteries immediately and store them in the case. The film can trap moisture and cause corrosion.*

---

### 5. Optional: Mechanical Keyboard Cover (For Backpack Use)

**Why you might need it:** If you frequently toss your calculator into a crammed backpack with hard edges (like a laptop or binder), a **silicone keyboard cover** adds an extra layer of dust and impact protection under the hard case. It prevents keys from getting jammed by crumbs or sand.

**What to look for:**
- **Model-specific:** Only buy a cover designed for your exact calculator model. General “scientific calculator” covers won’t fit the button layout.
- **Thickness:** A **thin silicone skin** (0.5mm–1mm) is best—it shouldn’t interfere with key travel or slide-on case fitting.
- **Anti-slip:** Look for a cover with a textured surface on the back to grip the calculator body.
- **No sticky residue:** Choose medical-grade silicone. Cheap PVC covers can leave a sticky film on the keys.

**Pro tip:** *Install the cover only when inserting into the hard case for backpack travel. Remove it during exams to avoid tactile interference.*
